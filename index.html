A modern AI pattern, in miniature

This page demonstrates the core ideas behind production assistants — small enough to audit end-to-end.

1 · Retrieval (the R in RAG)

Your message is tokenized and scored against every section of the knowledge base using BM25, the ranking function behind classic search engines. Top sections win by term frequency, rarity across the corpus, and length normalization.

2 · Grounded generation (the G)

Large language models generate prose and can invent facts ("hallucinate"). This demo takes the opposite approach: it composes extractively, copying approved sentences verbatim and citing the document each one came from. Nothing can be invented — which is why the confidence meter can be trusted.

3 · Agent reasoning

Instead of a single call, an agent loop decides what to do: scan for danger → classify intent → plan tools → search → gate by relevance → compose → attach safety layers. Every decision lands in the reasoning trace you can expand under each answer. An LLM-based assistant would slot into the "compose" step — the plumbing around it stays the same.

4 · Guardrails

A safety layer wraps both ends. On the way in: crisis and active-emergency detection outrank everything. On the way out: escalation banners, an honest "no approved guidance found" refusal below the relevance threshold, and a standing disclaimer. Scope is enforced too — out-of-domain requests are declined.

5 · Why show all this?

Transparency is a safety feature. When a system explains what it retrieved, why, and how confident it is, users can catch its mistakes — the most important control of all.

Privacy controls

  • Zero network egress. The page makes no requests after loading — verifiable in your browser's DevTools Network tab. It even works offline.
  • Zero persistence. No cookies, no localStorage. The conversation exists only in this tab's memory.
  • One-click erasure. "Clear chat" wipes the transcript instantly.
  • User-initiated export. "Export chat" writes a local file on your device only.
  • PII redaction in traces. Phone numbers, emails, and street addresses are masked before being echoed anywhere.

Safety controls

  • Emergency-first ordering. Detected emergencies put "call 112 now" at the very top, before any guidance.
  • Crisis-line pinning. Self-harm indicators trigger a dedicated response with human crisis lines (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, 988, Samaritans) — never just first-aid text.
  • Abstention. Below the relevance threshold the bot refuses instead of guessing: "I could not find approved guidance…" with next-step suggestions.
  • Verbatim sourcing. Every guidance line is copied from a reviewed document, with its review date one click away.
  • Scope enforcement. Requests outside emergency guidance are politely declined.

Known limits

  • Small corpus (… documents, … sections) — many valid questions will correctly return "no approved guidance".
  • Lexical search with synonym expansion, not true semantic understanding — unusual phrasings may still miss. Detected emergencies always get the universal first-steps checklist even then.
  • Understands English and common Hinglish emergency phrasings. Not a medical device, not reviewed by a medical authority — a demonstration of architecture.
